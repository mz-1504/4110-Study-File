# ELI4110 Course Summary (Modules 1–6)

## Metadata

**Course:** ELI4110 (The Ethical Engineer)
**Modules:** 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6
**Key Themes:** Professionalism, Canadian Legal System, PE Act & Regulation 941 (Misconduct vs. Ethics), Tort Law, Contract Law (Ron Engineering), Procurement, Intellectual Property, Workplace Ethics.

## High-yield outline

*   **Professional Governance (Ontario):** Self-regulation model via PEO. Main object: Protect public interest.
*   **Legal Framework:**
    *   **PE Act:** Statute law; establishes PEO, licensure requirements.
    *   **Reg. 941:** Rules for conduct/discipline. s.72 = Misconduct (enforceable); s.77 = Code of Ethics (ideal).
    *   **Discipline:** PEO Discipline Committee hears cases; enforceability relies on s.72.
*   **Tort Law:**
    *   Civil wrong compensating for loss/injury.
    *   **3 Essentials:** Duty of Care + Breach of Duty + Causation (Injury/Damage).
    *   **Standard:** "Reasonable and prudent practitioner."
    *   **Vicarious Liability:** Employer liable for employee's negligence.
*   **Contract Law:**
    *   **5 Essentials:** Offer/Acceptance, Mutual Intent, Consideration, Capacity, Lawful Purpose.
    *   **Contract Interpretation:** *Contra Proferentem* (ambiguity against drafter).
    *   **Discharge:** Performance, Agreement, Frustration (unforeseeable event).
*   **Procurement (Tendering):**
    *   **Ron Engineering:** Submission of valid bid = **Contract A** (rules of tender). Awarding job = **Contract B** (construction contract).
    *   **Irrevocable offers:** Must have consideration (or seal) to be binding.
*   **Intellectual Property:**
    *   **Copyright:** Life + 70 years (orig. work).
    *   **Patent:** 20 years (novel, useful, inventive).
    *   **Trademark:** 10 years (renewable).
*   **Workplace Ethics:**
    *   **Conflict of Interest:** Actual, Perceived, or Potential. Must disclose.
    *   **Whistleblowing:** 1. Discuss with supervisor (unless dangerous), 2. Escalate, 3. Regulatory authority (PEO).
    *   **Employment:** Engineer = Agent/Technical Authority; Employer = Management Authority.

## Key definitions

| Term | Precise Definition | Common Confusion | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fiduciary Duty** | Heightened duty to act in priority to another's interest (trust relationship). | Not all contracts create fiduciary duty. | Director of a corporation; Physician/Patient. |
| **Consideration** | Something of value exchanged by parties (essential for contract). | Does not have to be money; can be a promise. | "I promise to pay $50 for this service." |
| **Gratuitous Promise** | A promise made without consideration. | Generally unenforceable unless relied upon (Estoppel). | "I'll give you a ride" (cancelling later isn't breach). |
| **Equitable Estoppel** | Prevents a party from enforcing strict rights if they allowed a change and the other party relied on it. | Confused with contract modification. | Landlord accepts late rent for months; can't suddenly evict for late rent without notice. |
| **Quantum Meruit** | "As much as is deserved" – payment for services where price wasn't fixed. | Confused with damages. | Consultant does work without signed contract; court awards fair market rate. |
| **Tort** | Civil wrong entitling victim to damages for negligence/loss. | distinct from Criminal (State) or Contract (Agreement). | Engineer miscalculates beam; building collapses. |
| **Vicarious Liability** | Employer is liable for negligence of employee in performance of job. | Employee is still personally liable too. | Firm pays for Engineer's design error. |
| **Contract A** | Contract *to* tender; arises when compliant bid is submitted. | Confused with the final construction contract. | Bidder forfeits deposit if they refuse to sign Contract B. |
| **Repudiation** | One party refuses to perform an essential requirement of the contract. | Confused with simple breach. | Contractor walks off site halfway through. |
| **Industrial Design** | Protection of shape, pattern, or ornamentation of an object. | Distinct from utility (Patent). | The specific curve of a bottle. |

## Rules / tests / elements

### Negligence (Tort Action) - 3 Elements
Plaintiff must prove on balance of probabilities:
1.  **Duty of Care:** Defendant owed plaintiff a duty (Legal obligation).
2.  **Breach of Duty:** Conduct fell below standard of a "reasonable person/practitioner."
3.  **Causation:** Defendant's conduct caused injury/loss to plaintiff.

### Contract Formation - 5 Elements
1.  Offer and Acceptance.
2.  Mutual Intent (to enter legal relations).
3.  Consideration (Exchange of value).
4.  Capacity (Age, mental state, authority).
5.  Lawful Purpose.

### Tendering (Ron Engineering Test)
1.  **Invitation to Tender:** Offer by Owner to consider bids.
2.  **Submission of Bid:** Acceptance by Bidder. Creates **Contract A**.
    *   *Terms of Contract A:* Bidder cannot withdraw; Owner must treat all bidders fairly.
3.  **Award:** Creates **Contract B** (the project contract).

### Professional Misconduct (Reg 941 s.72)
Includes (but not limited to):
*   Negligence (substandard work).
*   Failure to make reasonable provision for safeguarding life/health/property.
*   Failure to correct/report situations endangering safety/welfare.
*   Failure to disclose conflict of interest (promptly, voluntarily, fully).
*   Sealing documents not prepared/checked by practitioner.
*   Harassment.

## Must-know statutes / codes / clauses

*   **Professional Engineers Act (R.S.O. 1990, c. P.28):**
    *   *Governs:* The PEO, licensing, discipline.
    *   *Trigger:* Practicing engineering in Ontario.
*   **Regulation 941, Section 72:**
    *   *Governs:* **Professional Misconduct**.
    *   *Trigger:* Disciplinary hearings (enforceable penalties: fines, revocation).
*   **Regulation 941, Section 77:**
    *   *Governs:* **Code of Ethics**.
    *   *Trigger:* General guidance on duty to public (paramount), employer, client, profession, self. (Note: Breach of s.77 isn't misconduct *unless* it violates s.72, though s.72(h) links them).
*   **Industrial Exception (PE Act s.12(3)a):**
    *   *Governs:* Unlicensed practice.
    *   *Trigger:* Designing machinery for use in employer's *own* production facility (exempt from needing P.Eng).
*   **Limitation Clauses:**
    *   *Governs:* Contract liability.
    *   *Trigger:* Contract breach. Limits damages (e.g., "not liable for consequential damages").

## Case patterns / examples mentioned

*   **Ron Engineering (1981):** Contractor made $750k error. Tried to withdraw. **Ruling:** Deposit forfeited. Submission created "Contract A" (unilateral contract).
*   **Donoghue v. Stevenson (1932):** Snail in ginger beer. **Ruling:** Manufacturer owes Duty of Care to consumer despite no direct contract. Established modern negligence.
*   **Hedley Byrne v. Heller (1963):** Bank gave bad credit reference "without responsibility." **Ruling:** Negligent misrepresentation can cause economic loss. (Bank saved by disclaimer, but principle established).
*   **Quebec Bridge (1907/1916):** Collapse due to design/weight errors. **Lesson:** Leadership failures, lack of oversight, "organizational deviance."
*   **Elliot Lake Mall (2012):** Roof collapse. **Lesson:** Duty to report/act on corrosion. Resulted in recommendations to strengthen PEO powers.
*   **Amazon.com (1-click Patent):** **Ruling:** Business methods *can* be patented in Canada if they contain a novel technical element.
*   **BIDCO (Scenario):** Submitted bid with $1M error. **Result:** Typically bound by Contract A; owner keeps deposit if Bidco refuses Contract B.
*   **Clearwater v. Pulverized Pulp:** Equipment removed only 70% of waste (spec 95%). **Result:** Simple breach (not fundamental). Owner gets damages (cost to fix), but cannot repudiate (cancel) whole contract. Limitation clause blocked consequential damages (lost profits).

## Common exam traps

*   **Duty to Public:** It is paramount (s.77.2.i), but you must still balance confidentiality unless danger is imminent/illegal.
*   **Misconduct vs. Ethics:** Writing an exam answer citing s.77 (Ethics) for a discipline case is weak. Cite **s.72 (Misconduct)** for enforceable breaches.
*   **Contract A Revocability:** An offer is only irrevocable if it has **consideration** or a **seal**. Tenders are usually sealed.
*   **Manager vs. Leader:** Slides emphasize "Manager" is authority/direction; "Leader" is inspiration/vision. Not synonyms.
*   **Whistleblowing:** Do not go to the media first. 1. Supervisor -> 2. Senior Mgmt -> 3. PEO/Regulators.
*   **Standard of Care:** The test is **not** perfection. It is "reasonable and prudent practitioner" at the time.
*   **Copyright:** Does not require registration (automatic upon creation). Registration just helps proof.

## Mini scenarios

**1. The Silent Supplier**
*   **Scenario:** Engineer specifies pumps from a company owned by their spouse without telling the client. The pumps are the best price and quality.
*   **Issue:** Conflict of Interest (Actual/Perceived).
*   **What rule applies?** Reg 941 s.72(2)(i) - Failure to make prompt, voluntary, complete disclosure of interest.
*   **Best action:** Disclose immediately to client.
*   **Why?** Even if quality is good, lack of disclosure is Professional Misconduct. Prejudicial to judgment.

**2. The Forgotten Seal**
*   **Scenario:** A P.Eng is busy and asks a junior EIT to finalize and seal a design drawing the P.Eng hasn't reviewed.
*   **Issue:** Misuse of seal / Negligence.
*   **What rule applies?** Reg 941 s.72(2)(e) - Signing/sealing documents not actually prepared or checked by practitioner.
*   **Best action:** P.Eng must review the work thoroughly before sealing.
*   **Why?** Sealing indicates professional responsibility. Skipping review is Misconduct.

**3. The Low Bid Mistake**
*   **Scenario:** Contractor submits a sealed tender for $2M. Realizes 10 mins later they forgot $500k in costs. Owner accepts bid.
*   **Issue:** Contract A / Mistake.
*   **What rule applies?** Ron Engineering principle (Contract A formed on submission).
*   **Best action:** Contractor must perform or forfeit bid bond.
*   **Why?** Tender was irrevocable. Owner accepts "Contract A" by receiving compliant bid.

**4. The Leaky Roof**
*   **Scenario:** Engineer inspects a mall, finds severe rust, writes a report saying "urgent repair needed," gives it to owner. Owner ignores it. Engineer does nothing further.
*   **Issue:** Duty to Report / Public Safety.
*   **What rule applies?** Reg 941 s.72(2)(c) - Failure to correct or report situation endangering public safety.
*   **Best action:** Report to Chief Building Official or Ministry (since owner ignored).
*   **Why?** Duty to public welfare (s.77.2.i) overrides confidentiality when safety is at risk.

**5. The Helpful Neighbor**
*   **Scenario:** Engineer offers free advice to a neighbor about a load-bearing wall. Wall collapses.
*   **Issue:** Tort Liability / Duty of Care.
*   **What rule applies?** Hedley Byrne (Reliance on special skill).
*   **Best action:** Do not give off-hand professional advice, or explicit disclaimer.
*   **Why?** Duty of care exists if reliance is foreseeable, even without a contract/payment.

**6. The Copyrighted Code**
*   **Scenario:** Engineer writes software for Employer A. Moves to Employer B and uses snippets of that code to solve a similar problem.
*   **Issue:** Intellectual Property / Copyright.
*   **What rule applies?** Copyright Act (Work for hire).
*   **Best action:** Do not use the code. Rewrite from scratch.
*   **Why?** Employer A owns the copyright (work done during employment). Using it is infringement.

**7. The "Strict" Spec**
*   **Scenario:** Contract says "Project completed by June 1." Contractor finishes June 2. Owner tries to cancel payment for the whole project.
*   **Issue:** Breach of Contract (Simple vs. Fundamental).
*   **What rule applies?** Substantial Compliance.
*   **Best action:** Owner pays contract price minus damages for 1-day delay.
*   **Why?** This is a simple breach, not fundamental. Does not deprive owner of "substantially all benefit." Repudiation not allowed.

**8. The Copy-Paste Design**
*   **Scenario:** Engineer uses a design from a previous project for a new site with different soil, without checking.
*   **Issue:** Negligence.
*   **What rule applies?** Tort Law (Breach of Standard of Care) / Reg 941 s.72(2)(a).
*   **Best action:** Conduct proper analysis for specific site conditions.
*   **Why?** Failure to maintain standards of a "prudent practitioner."

## Ultra-short recall list

1.  **Plaintiff:** Party suing in civil court.
2.  **Crown:** Prosecutor in criminal court.
3.  **Tort:** Civil wrong, compensation for loss.
4.  **Balance of Probabilities:** Burden of proof in civil/tort cases.
5.  **Beyond Reasonable Doubt:** Burden of proof in criminal cases.
6.  **Duty of Care:** Obligation to avoid injuring others (Tort).
7.  **Standard of Care:** Reasonable and prudent practitioner.
8.  **Strict Liability:** Liability without needing to prove fault (e.g., Product Liability in US).
9.  **Vicarious Liability:** Employer liable for employee.
10. **Concurrent Tortfeasors:** Multiple defendants share fault.
11. **Limitation Period:** Time limit to sue (usually 2 years from discovery in Ontario).
12. **Offer:** Promise to do X for Y.
13. **Consideration:** Value exchanged (money, promise, service).
14. **Contract A:** The Bidding Contract (arises on submission).
15. **Contract B:** The Project Contract (arises on award).
16. **Irrevocable Offer:** Requires consideration or seal.
17. **Force Majeure:** Clause excusing performance due to "Acts of God."
18. **Contra Proferentem:** Ambiguity interpreted against the drafter.
19. **Equitable Estoppel:** Can't enforce rights you clearly waived.
20. **Quantum Meruit:** Payment for value of work done (no price set).
21. **Specific Performance:** Court orders party to fulfill contract (rare).
22. **Injunction:** Court orders party to stop doing something.
23. **Fundamental Breach:** Deprives innocent party of substantially all benefit.
24. **Simple Breach:** Minor failure; warrants damages, not cancellation.
25. **Liquidated Damages:** Pre-agreed penalty in contract.
26. **Reg 941 s.72:** Professional Misconduct (The "Don'ts").
27. **Reg 941 s.77:** Code of Ethics (The "Shoulds").
28. **Paramount Duty:** To public welfare.
29. **Conflict of Interest:** Must disclose *promptly, voluntarily, completely*.
30. **Industrial Exception:** No license needed for designs for employer's internal use.
31. **Patent:** Protects invention (20 yrs).
32. **Trademark:** Protects brand/logo (10 yrs, renewable).
33. **Copyright:** Protects expression of idea (Life + 70 yrs).
34. **Moral Rights:** Author's right to integrity/attribution (cannot sell, only waive).
35. **Trade Secret:** Protection via confidentiality/non-disclosure (no registration).
36. **Functional Org:** Grouped by specialty (e.g., all mechanicals together).
37. **Projectized Org:** Grouped by project (dedicated resources).
38. **Matrix Org:** Dual reporting (Functional Manager + Project Manager).
39. **Moral Machine:** Study showing preferences in autonomous vehicle ethics.
40. **Learned Intermediary:** Professional must warn client of product risks.
